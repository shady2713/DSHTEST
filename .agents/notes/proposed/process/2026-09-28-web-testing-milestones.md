## Problem

The full scope needs a construction order that tests uncertain integration and product effectiveness before dependent implementation.

Existing results keep their original scope.

This file defines what to do first, its dependencies, and the evidence needed to proceed. **Implementation order and stage exit criteria**

Every stage delivers the actual baseline, changes, applicable official checks, normal and failure controls, and incomplete items. Stage demonstrations may cover only some features but must state that scope. Stage acceptance is not product delivery. Tested-source protection, environment authorization, action deduplication, and state preservation apply from the first actual action; safety and recovery cannot be deferred until later.

Budgets, capability matrix, reports, and known limitations agree; unverified required capabilities do not count as delivered. |

Policy restrictions apply from M1, not only after a separate package is complete. The first package must not prebuild every final abstraction. Ordinary DSH conversation works in M1; Skill drafts/confirmation and project analysis arrive incrementally in M2–M4. Complete Skills capability still follows the original acceptance requirements.

**M0: bounded preliminary technical validation**

M0 attempts to falsify key assumptions and establish product effectiveness; it is not a reduced product. P01–P05 each have at most 8 active engineering hours, P06 at most 16, and P07/P08 at most 8 each: 72 hours for the initial eight probes. Preparation, independent answers, and conclusion work are separate; external waiting is listed separately. This initial validation timebox is not a completion promise, model-cost allowance, or user test-task limit. At startup, record owner, version, device, start/end, and budget. Upgrade integration and regression of upstream fixes such as #4595/#5214 count toward P05. If more time is needed, record remaining questions, time used, and an extension/reallocation decision; do not promise completion within 72 hours. The main agent may adjust engineering arrangements within authorized development scope; changes to scope, baseline, or protection level require a separate decision. Do not repeatedly reset the clock or label an overrun as a pass.

At its deadline, each probe must produce ProbeResult: hypothesis, version/environment, operations, failure controls, actual commands/evidence, elapsed time, modified scope, conclusion, and downstream dependencies. Insufficient evidence at timeout means deferred, not proven failure or success. Before further probing, specify remaining questions and an additional timebox. If a solution expands baseline changes or alters product commitments, present the concrete tradeoff instead of silently deleting features, removing read-only protection, or replacing DSH.

M0-T08 may record M0 as unpassed while permitting only the [listed M1 subitems](2026-09-28-web-testing-tasks-m1.md#development-admission) whose prerequisite evidence is verified. The integrator records consumed evidence and stopping conditions per subitem; this is neither blanket M1 admission nor permission to bypass browser prerequisites. M0 run artifacts are not full AC acceptance. Probe code retained in the formal project must still satisfy official ownership, types, tests, documentation, and checks; keep no temporary bypasses or parallel agent-loop.

**P06: bounded product-effectiveness criteria**

P06 runs in M0-T09 without requiring complete M2/M4 implementation. Reuse the accepted minimal DSH assembly, model, controlled execution, and persistence boundaries to complete analysis through reporting for a limited business subset. Do not prebuild the final framework or disable protection for demonstrations. Retained probe code must still pass official checks. P06 findings join P07/P08 findings in constraining M0-T08.

Before execution, the acceptance party fixes ProductEffectivenessProtocol: at least one legally usable project snapshot not tailored to this tool, an explicit limited business subset, initial data/reset procedure, correct/defective controls, user-supplied rules, feature/defect inventories known only to acceptance, individual severities, and verification count/order. Include implementation/intent disagreement, rules not yet supplied, visual/interaction faults, and normal behavior. Answers must not be ordinary project files or model inputs; the execution Agent must not read acceptance logs or defect inventories. Separate calibration/correction fixtures from held-out validation. After seeing answers and making corrections, use fresh held-out controls for a continuation decision.

Record identified features/preregistered required features, detected/known defects, false positives, erroneous business passes, unverified items, user clarification count, active minutes spent preparing/taking over/reviewing, total elapsed time, and model usage. Asking the correct question about an unknown requirement is not defect detection; after user confirmation, actually retest before counting detection. The human reference uses the same scope, data, operations, and report requirements; record measurement order and familiarity. Report first-run preparation and same-version regression costs separately, and do not invent benefits without an actual human measurement. An unfamiliar project's original version must not be tailored to this tool. Defective controls may be prepared in separate development fixture copies; product execution still cannot modify tested source.

Initial continuation criteria apply only to this preregistered small sample: identify every required feature and include it in cases; after necessary rules are supplied, identify every required known defect with evidence; produce no false positives on correct controls, no erroneous business pass for unconfirmed intent, and no hidden gaps among mandatory checks. The user deferred the human-reference comparison to M5; retain it as an unverified effectiveness criterion, not an M0 pass. Before final acceptance, at least one same-scope regression must require less active user time than the human reference; report machine time separately. If any criterion fails, revise/defer. Authorized local corrections and independent probes may continue, but dependent full construction is not admitted. Do not claim that a small sample proves zero missed defects on all projects. M2/M4 expand held-out fixtures; M5 still accepts against all ACs. Freeze the sample and criteria before execution; do not remove defects, alter correct answers, or narrow scope after seeing results.

**Acceptance fixture construction plan**

F1–F6 are six fixture categories built from composable small examples and one representative business system, not six complete applications. Each has a version, deterministic initial data, expected results, known defects, correct controls, a reset procedure, and applicable IDs. Acceptance maintains the expected inventory independently; the tool must not generate its own answer key. Planted-defect answers are not ordinary project requirement inputs.

| Fixture | Earliest use and incremental expansion | Independent evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| F1 pages and input | M0 supplies minimal click, input, file-dialog, and two-role examples; before M4, add complex pages, IME, normal/abnormal visuals, and every viewport. | Interaction events, target state, correct layout, and correct/incorrect results for identical input. |
| F2 complete business system | M0/M1 prepares a minimal two-role create–approve–view flow; M2–M4 adds multiple directories/entry points, rejection, and collaborative editing. | Valid state changes for the same record, role visibility, correct/planted-defect versions, and representative source fixtures in different languages. |
| F3 backend and time | M2 establishes asynchronous entry points, logs/read-only verification; M4 adds real time windows and terminal failure states. | Acceptance is not completion; due-time rules and observations distinguish simulated time from actual elapsed time. |
| F4 history and versions | Retain genuinely produced assets from M1 onward; M2–M4 creates two business versions and samples of old attachments, rules, cookies/cache. | Original-version origins, changed/unchanged controls, and compatibility expectations; newly generated data cannot masquerade as history. |
| F5 runtime faults | Establish interruption points in M0; M3 fills out storage, model, notification, space, stalling, and late-return faults. | Whether the fault was actually applied, whether the target acted, persistence boundaries, and external state after recovery. |
| F6 optional specialized tests | M4 prepares controlled performance/load and access-control scenarios; M5 verifies the tool's specialized capabilities. | Explicit targets and known issues/correct controls; real production services are not fault fixtures. |

Prepare fixtures early in parallel with implementation in separate directories, keeping public inputs separate from hidden Oracle answers. Real history must come from earlier-stage artifacts; parallel preparation does not permit fabrication. Experiments sharing a target environment, native desktop, control root, or resource-acceptance machine run sequentially to avoid contamination. Development examples and fault injection belong in the official test system. When testing user projects, the product still cannot modify tested source, install dependencies, or deploy old versions itself. Controlled substitutes may represent third-party providers in fault-classification tests, but cannot replace every required acceptance of real requests, DSH Loader, storage, and actual executors.

**Measuring and freezing resource budgets**

V07's “bounded memory” must become the measurable criteria below. Measure and propose candidates in M0/M1; freeze ResourceBudgetRevision for the implemented combination before M3 ends. Measure new M4 routes separately and freeze the complete combination before M5. Do not start or declare success for M5's 24-hour test with missing numeric thresholds or thresholds changed after execution. Historical measurements do not yet establish a frozen budget for the selected application combination; audit their sampling and inputs before reuse. The table lists required fields without invented GB or handle limits.

| Scope | Parameters to fix before execution | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Reference environment | Windows build, CPU, RAM, disk type/free space, DSH/dependency versions, viewport/DPI, concurrent pages/roles, recording mode, fixture size, and random seed | Results apply to the listed configuration. Retain native evidence for both the minimum supported system and a representative Win11 system; the reference machine is not the only supported device. |
| Memory | Peak private-bytes limit across all managed processes, a separate Host limit, warm-up duration, sampling interval, stable equal-load window, and permitted growth slope | Include Main, Host, Renderer, managed standalone browsers, and required native processes; account for user services separately. Retain the full time series, not just the last value; explicitly record missing samples. |
| Handles and resources | Per-process peak handles/threads, maximum active targets/child processes, release waiting period, and allowed residue after repeated create/destroy cycles | Evaluate complete lifecycle controls. Legitimate runtime caches are not automatically leaks but need explicit bounds and explanations. |
| Disk | Upper bounds on record/evidence growth by capture mode, artifact budget per action/minute, allowed unreferenced temporary material, warning headroom, reserved control-record space, and write-check interval | Distinguish reasonable accumulation of necessary evidence from unexplained growth. Protective waiting starts before critical state can no longer be saved. Measure recording off/on separately; do not delete evidence to meet metrics. |
| Persistence and control | Opening/query/commit/recovery time limits at fixed record scales, and maximum latency from local control input to dispatch prohibition | Record sample counts, percentiles, and worst cases. Separate model/business waiting from local control time and include explicit disk-failure controls. |

These engineering budgets limit uncontrolled tool resource use; they are not cumulative cost or duration limits on user tasks. The user may omit a storage cap, but space warnings and save-capacity protection must be configured and verified. If a budget is exceeded, adjust implementation or clearly propose a device/commitment change; do not raise thresholds after acceptance to manufacture a pass. The implementation owner proposes budgets from measurements, independent acceptance checks raw series/workloads/criteria, and the main agent freezes them before subsequent formal runs. Implementers cannot change thresholds alone and declare themselves passed.

**Deliverables and change management**

Final delivery includes stage evidence, M0 probe conclusions, fixture-version inventory, ResourceBudgetRevision, actual DD02 browser capability-matrix results, requirement traceability, and release composition. Maintain the capability matrix only in [Interfaces and execution DD02](../architecture/2026-09-28-web-testing-design-execution.md), not another support-status table here.

Storage segmentation, checkpoint frequency, and internal abstractions are adjustable implementation candidates. M0 may simplify them while preserving domain authority, recovery semantics, evidence retention, and DSH rules. Update detailed design and affected acceptance first; do not retain proven-unnecessary structures just to preserve an old diagram. Follow [Upstream baseline and upgrades](2026-09-28-web-testing-upstream-baseline.md); exploration does not lower applicable engineering checks.

Resume by checking current evidence and recording T08's limited-subitem decision; restore the Electron runtime, then complete P01's finite broker/carrier comparison and P03 lifecycle. Independently revalidate affected P02/P05/P07/P08 evidence, close P06 coverage gaps, and implement eligible M1 subitems. Resolve any reproduced Loader/model-request failure without turning a historical failure into a permanent prerequisite. Revisit full T08 admission only when the required evidence is ready. Preserve original time spent; the integrator records any additional or reallocated timebox, questions, and stopping points within existing development authorization. Do not treat the earlier 15–25-day estimate as measured cost or a completion promise.

## Alternatives considered

**Recorded choice.** Building every subsystem at once leaves critical assumptions untested. A stage pass cannot substitute for final product acceptance.

## Acceptance criteria

Execute this proposal’s normal and failure controls and satisfy the [shared acceptance criteria](../testing/2026-09-28-web-testing-acceptance.md) and applicable task evidence requirements. Documentation migration does not establish a pass.

## Risks

Probe timeboxes can expose an infeasible route; expiry requires an explicit continue, adjust, or defer decision rather than silent scope reduction.
